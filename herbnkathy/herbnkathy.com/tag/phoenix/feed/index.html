It’s Halloween and were headed back to the Roost for the winter.

We’ve enjoyed spending the summer in Ohio and having the opportunity to spend time with our “kids” and the rest of the family.

We spent a few days with Betsy and Bob both up at their home in Owosso, and then a couple days with them in the early fall visiting The Henry Ford Museum in Dearborn Michigan. We took one afternoon while we were visiting “The Ford” and drove to downtown Detroit to reminisce where both Bob and I had worked so many years ago. 

Back in the early 70’s Bob had worked at the Detroit Stock Exchange in the Penobscot Building (top floor) while I had been a Xerox Service Tech working in the Penobscot, Buhl, and Guardian buildings.

We took a walk down to the riverfront and were planning on visiting the recently renovated Michigan Central Train Station (closed since the late 80’s) but it was closed for continued work in the lobby. Ford Motor Company bought the vacant and vandalized building a few years ago and has been in the process of restoring it to its original beauty while intending to make it their new technology center.

I’ve been keeping busy this summer by working 3 days a week at Morrow County Area Transit taking folks to medical appts and local shopping trips.  It keeps me out of trouble Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays while Kathy keeps busy taking care of the household chores.

As I write this we are on the plane for the 4 hour trip from Flint, Michigan to Mesa, Arizona.

“But I thought you lived in Ohio?” you say.  Yes, but we want to fly Allegiant Air because of its low cost.  Depending on the day you fly, you can go for as low as $39.  We wanted a direct low-cost flight and although Allegiant flies out of Columbus, Toledo, and Cincinnati, none of those flights fly direct to Phoenix Mesa Gateway airport which is a much smaller airport than Phoenix Sky Harbor.

Since Betsy and Bob live in Owosso and it’s only about 40 minutes from Flint where Allegiant flies direct to Mesa, the choice was obvious.

We drove the 3 hours yesterday from Mt Gilead to Owosso, had a wonderful dinner with Bob and Bets, woke up at 0 dark thirty this morning and they drove us to the airport.  Our car will be in their neighbors extra garage for the winter snug as a bug in a rug!

Our plane touched down at about 9am Arizona time and we took an Uber for the 30 minute ride down to the storage facility where our car was waiting for us.  After starting the car and using the windshield wipers to clear the desert dust off the windshield, we went to the car wash around the corner.

So far so good and were looking forward to a warm and fun few months in Arizona.

]]> https://herbnkathy.com/2024/12/30/heading-back-for-the-winter/feed/ 0 Replacing Our Motorhome Windshield (Again) https://herbnkathy.com/2021/11/29/replacing-our-motorhome-windshield-again/ https://herbnkathy.com/2021/11/29/replacing-our-motorhome-windshield-again/#comments Mon, 29 Nov 2021 22:05:07 +0000 https://herbnkathy.com/?p=8243 Continue reading Replacing Our Motorhome Windshield (Again)]]> This is the second time we’ve replaced our motorhome windshield. The first was right after we bought it in 2016.

We had developed a stress crack in the upper right corner of the driver’s windshield the first time. But this time while we developed another stress crack (in the same location), we also had two large long cracks that had started as a result of rocks hitting the windshield.

When I first noticed one of the long cracks we were on our way from Missouri to South Dakota. It seemed to be getting longer, but I couldn’t be sure so I put a small piece of scotch tape at the end of the crack on the inside so I could monitor how fast it might be moving. And it was moving right along! You can see the tape in the picture above.

We put in a claim with our insurance company and they promptly ordered the correct glass from a company called Custom Glass Solutions in Ohio and placed the install order with Chris at Arizona RV Glass in Phoenix. When the glass arrived at Chris’ shop in Phoenix, I got the call to schedule installation.

This video shows the entire removal and install process from start to finish

Chris explained to me that stress cracks like the one we had in the upper right corner are pretty common. This is caused by too much flexing of the coach chassis. The flexing can be caused when trying to level the coach on an uneven site or when entering/exiting a fuel stop and driving diagonally across the bump at the curb … This causes the chassis to twist and as a result put too much strain on the windshield corners. He suggests to always try to turn in head on if possible.

We’ll try to be more careful from now on, but often it’s just not possible to miss all the potholes and bumps you come across on the open road.

In many older motorhomes like ours the windshield curves around the corners as compared with many of the newer motorhomes I’ve noticed the windshield seems to be more “flat” all across. I think maybe this curve around may be contributing to the problem.

I just hope this doesn’t happen too often … even with comprehensive coverage on our auto insurance policy, this can get pretty expensive over time between deductibles and premium increases. I called around before placing the claim and if I were to buy the windshield outright and pay for installation ourselves prices ranged from a low of $1700 to a high of $2300!

On another subject, we’re heading to Yuma tomorrow so that we can easily walk across the border into Los Algodones. My plan is to spend some time visiting the eye doctor and getting new eyeglasses along with picking up some prescription drugs at one of the pharmacies. We will also enjoy visiting our friends Paul and Chris who are spending the winter at the Escapees KOFA RV Park in Yuma. Our friends Jim and LuAnn left us here at the Roost this morning and we will meet up with them at KOFA as well. Looking forward to the six of us having a good time!

Until next time, take care of each other (and yourself) and we wish you well.

Herb & Kathy
